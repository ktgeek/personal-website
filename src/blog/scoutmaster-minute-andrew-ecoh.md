## Intro

Yesterday was one of my scout's Eagle Court of Honor. Every scout designs their count of honor differently, and this
might be the first time I was asked to give a Scoutmaster Minute at one. Because I've known Andrew so long and so well,
I wanted to make sure I put something together that was meaningful and personal and ends with a call to action as many
good Scoutmaster Minutes do.

I did however take the first little bit to NOT deliver my main message, but to recognize Andrew's mother, Cyndi, our
Troop Committee Chair.

This isn't exactly what I delivered as I adlib and add things as they happened in the day, but this was what I wrote
originally.

## Scoutmaster Minute - Andrew's Eagle Court of Honor - Aug 30, 2026

Ok, because I know there are some smart alecks in here who like to time me during Scoutmaster Minutes… buckle in. This
is gonna be a long one, and way, way over a minute.

But before I get to the Scoutmaster Minute… even though tonight is a celebration for and about Andrew, I want to
recognize again someone who has been there with him every step of the way, his mother, Cyndi Melone. Cyndi has been our
troop's committee chair for 12 years, and a great partner (and boss) to me the whole time I've been Scoutmaster. She has
done it through Nick's years in the troop, through the years in between when she didn't have a Scout here at all, and
then through Andrew's years. In all that time, Cyndi has helped provide and maintain an environment where our Scouts can
thrive, learn, and grow. Most importantly, Cyndi has made sure we hold to one of the core principles of Scouting, being
Scout led.

Cyndi also started a tradition for us leaders that I love and hope this troop keeps forever. This patrol patch I'm
wearing *&lt;rip off patch*&gt; is an Infinity Patrol patch. The current definition of the Infinity Patrol, as I
understand it, is an adult leader who does not have a Scout in the troop. Evan and I joined the Infinity Patrol on the
same day. He turned 18 and became an Assistant Scoutmaster, which meant I no longer had a Scout in the troop. So the two
of us are relatively new members. There are people in this room who were with Cyndi for many more of those years… Sheri
Taylor, Monique Sparacino, Dan Kubick, Joe J, Steve Dudek, Dan Trankler, quitters like Jeff DeBuhr or Andrea Kohl…
*&lt;mention whoever else is around&gt;*. And there has been a running joke in the Infinity Patrol that we are all under
contract, that we have to stay on as leaders until Andrew earned Eagle. So with that said… we don't need these anymore!
*&lt;everyone makes a show of turning in their patrol patches to Cyndi*&gt;

*&lt;snatch my patch back and put it back on my uniform*&gt; Ok, mine is going back on for now. In all seriousness, a
few of us really are wrapping up in the not too distant future, and they have earned every bit of that retirement. But
the Infinity Patrol has never really been about who is leaving. It's about the people who stay when they don't have to.

### And now on to my actual Scoutmaster Minute…

Andrew asked me to give a Scoutmaster Minute at his Eagle Court of Honor. I asked him if he had a topic in mind, and he
said, "You could do a topic on what it takes to be an Eagle or why an Eagle is important to the society."

Only about 7% of Scouts earn the rank of Eagle. Earlier, when Andrew was receiving his gift from the troop, I rattled
off a bunch of well known names who are Eagle Scouts and NESA members. I am not among them, and that is why I'm not sure
I'm the best qualified person to talk about this. I'm a Scoutmaster, and I've been lucky to be part of a lot of Scouts'
journeys to Eagle, including my own son's, but as a youth I only earned the rank of Life. (LIFE FOR LIFE!) Still,
between my years as a Scout, 14 years as an adult leader, and my time as Scoutmaster, I have a few thoughts.

One of the first things I think about with Eagle Scout is that it is always in the present tense. Nobody says they were
an Eagle Scout. They say they are one. The rank isn't a marker of something you finished, it's something that shapes
your character and your values for life. You will find yourself falling into leadership roles and helping get things
done. You'll find yourself performing service, even if it's as simple as picking up a piece of trash on your way across
a parking lot into a store.

I asked a few friends of mine who are Eagle Scouts if they had any thoughts on this topic.

My friend and former coworker Spencer Olson told me:

> I remember when I got Eagle, it was one of the first times in my life where I felt a significant sense of pride. You
> did so much hard stuff, dealt with unexpected turns, had to skip hanging out with friends, had to learn how to push
> through in moments when you were doubting yourself, had to learn to be a leader, etc... and then....YOU DID IT. You
> should feel an immense sense of pride. You will have to solve big problems like this for the rest of your life, and
> now you have your first blueprint. You can do hard things, push past obstacles, and you know the amazing feeling that
> comes when you're done (and you know all the ways that you helped others along the way).

Another Eagle told me:

> Being an Eagle isn't so much about having the award, it's about the journey that led you to it. Every step taken to
> Eagle you learn and grow, and while there may be setbacks along the way it's the perseverance that Eagles have that
> lets them take another step forward no matter what, because they want to be better, not just for themselves, but for
> the people they meet along the way.
>
> Eagle Scout is just a title, but that title carries a lot of weight and steps. That's what people see when they hear
> Eagle, 'a model Scout who has been on a journey and has succeeded.' What people don't see when they hear Eagle is the
> continuation an Eagle has after they reach it. An Eagle continues to step forward, as it's now a part of them to keep
> on going and learning.

In case anyone was wondering, that Eagle I just quoted is today's MC, Evan Garner.

As both Spencer and Evan get at, Andrew, you have already done a great many things to get here. But there is a duty
going forward.

Rob Ortiz, an Eagle Scout and an Assistant Scoutmaster in our troop, tells the story of walking down the street and
stepping in to defend someone who was being harassed. The harasser said, "What are you, some kind of Eagle Scout?" and
Rob said, "Yes. Yes, I am," and kept right on being an Upstander. Him speaking up got a few other people to step in too.

As Rob's example shows, this is about more than earning some merit badges and doing a service project. You have
dedicated yourself to living the Scout Oath and Law for your entire life, and to helping other people. You have chosen
to take on the mantle of being an Upstander, an active citizen in our country, and a lifelong leader in your community.
And as I have already watched you do in this troop, you have chosen to lead by example and to mentor the Scouts coming
up behind you.

Let's keep looking forward. As you finish up high school, you hear us adult leaders talk about how colleges take Eagle
into account. And it's true, they do. A couple of years ago Evan got proof of that. His acceptance letter from Marquette
said that as an Eagle Scout he would fit in there, because of shared values like service and Duty to God. For most Eagle
Scouts, this award also turns out to be the one item from their high school years that survives on a professional
résumé. Most 30 somethings looking for a job won't put "high school debate team runner up" or "MVP of the bowling team"
on there. But you can bet they'll include "Eagle Scout." I have done a lot of interviewing and hiring, and whenever I
see Eagle Scout on a résumé I ask about the project. How someone talks about that project, what they ran into and how
they got through it, tells me something that usually makes them an easy hire.

Finally, for you, Andrew, a request. Think about how you can give back to Scouting. You still have a few years as a
Scout in the Venturing Crew, so I hope you stay active there and in the Order of the Arrow as well. And peering a little
further into the future, maybe giving back won't happen right away, and that is ok. Maybe it comes when you have Scout
age children of your own. But look at Eagle Scout Mr. J as an example. It has been a while since his son was in the
troop, and he is still here, still contributing, still making this troop stronger.

Now, for the Scouts here who are Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, or Life rank… earlier today,
every Eagle in this room stood up front and rededicated themselves to the Eagle Charge by saying the Eagle Oath. You
probably only know a few of them. But the ones you do know, think about the example they have set, not just in how to be
a model Scout, but in how to be a leader, a mentor, and an all around good human. When you think about what you want out
of your time in Scouting, or about the decisions in front of you, whether you have Eagle as a goal or not, it wouldn't
hurt to ask yourself, "What would *&lt;insert your favorite Eagle Scout&gt;* do?" More importantly, remember that every
single one of them was once exactly where you are right now. They all had rank requirements, merit badges, and service
hours ahead of them. They all had sports, and homework, and plenty of distractions outside of Scouting. Their
experience, their perseverance, their leadership, and their ability to live the Scout Oath and Law are what led them to
Eagle. You are capable of that, and as Eagles like Neil Armstrong have shown us, so much more. I challenge you to learn
from Andrew and the others, and to keep walking that path. And when you need help or mentoring along the way, I know
they will be there for you.

And for the rest of you... nobody gets here alone. Andrew earned this. Nobody earned it for him. But somebody
drove him to meetings. Somebody signed a blue card. Three somebodies gave up a weeknight to sit on a board of review. A
dozen somebodies gave up a Saturday to work on an Eagle project for a Scout who was not their own kid. Every Eagle Scout
standing in this room today is standing on a pile of small yeses from adults who were under no obligation to say yes.

So that is my ask of everyone here. Say yes. Take the training. Sit on a board of review. Drive to camp. Ask a Scout you
barely know how his project is going, and then actually listen to the answer. And if you think you are off the hook
because you don't have a Scout in this troop, come talk to the Infinity Patrol. We are proof that it is the best reason
to stay, not a reason to leave.

Andrew, congratulations. You have made this troop better, and you have earned every bit of today.

As a country, as a world, we need more folks who dedicate themselves to the ideals of Scouting, to the Oath and the Law.
We need more Eagles. Scouts, we need you to keep climbing. Everyone else, we need you to help them get there.
